# Performance reviews and feedback

## Feedback model

Situation, behaviour, impact. Name the specific situation, describe the
behaviour that was observable, and state the effect it had. "In Tuesday's design
review you interrupted Priya twice, and she stopped contributing" is feedback.
"You need to be more collaborative" is not.

## No surprises

Nothing in a written review should be news to the person. If it is, I failed to
give the feedback when it happened. I keep a running document per person with
dated observations so the review covers the whole period and not just the last
six weeks.

## Calibration

Managers bring proposed ratings with evidence to a calibration session. The
purpose is consistency across teams, not fitting a curve. A rating changes only
when another manager brings evidence, not because of a quota.

## Underperformance

Start with a direct conversation and a written, specific expectation with a
date. Most performance problems are unclear expectations or a role mismatch.
A formal improvement plan is the last step, lasts 60 days, and has weekly
check-ins with written notes.

## Recognising strong performers

Tell them specifically what they did that mattered, give them a bigger problem
rather than more of the same work, and make their work visible to my manager.
