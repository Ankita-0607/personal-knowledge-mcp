# Sprint planning

## Capacity

Start from people-days, not story points. Take the working days in the sprint,
subtract leave and public holidays, then multiply by a focus factor of 0.7 to
account for meetings, reviews and support. Velocity is a sanity check on that
number, not the starting point.

## Commitment

We commit to a sprint goal, one sentence about the outcome, and treat the
ticket list as the current best plan for reaching it. If a ticket turns out not
to serve the goal, it can be swapped without the sprint having "failed".

## Carry-over

More than 20 percent of committed work carrying over two sprints in a row means
we are planning to a fantasy capacity. The fix is to commit to less, not to push
harder. Carried-over tickets are re-estimated, never assumed.

## Unplanned work

We reserve 15 percent of capacity for production support and urgent requests.
If the reserve is not used by the middle of the sprint, the team pulls the next
item from the top of the backlog.

## Planning meeting format

Ninety minutes at most. Product presents the goal and the top of the backlog,
the team asks questions, then the team sizes and commits without product in the
room for that last part. Refinement happens before planning, not during it.
